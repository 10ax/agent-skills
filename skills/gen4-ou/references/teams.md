## Roles — keep these straight

| Pokémon | Job | Do not use it for |
|---|---|---|
| **Hippowdon** | Sand, Stealth Rock, physical wall, **Roar to erase setup** | Special walling is its EV spread, but Grass Knot is 2× and it's heavy — worst possible switch into Infernape |
| **Gliscor** | **Fighting resist**, Taunt to shut off hazards and recovery | Anything special. Base 75 SpD uninvested — Fire Blast does ~70% |
| **Clefable** | **Seismic Toss** answer to CM Clefable/Suicune/Jirachi; the **only free pivot** | Nothing — this is the most valuable member |
| **Latias** | Defensive pivot; **the mixed Infernape answer**; Reflect support | A sweeper. This is a Bold Toxic/Reflect set, not a win condition |
| **Jirachi** | Wish support, para/flinch pressure, resists Grass and Dark | Tanking Fire Blast — Fire is 2× on Steel |
| **Magneton** | **Scalpel.** Traps and kills Skarmory (Thunderbolt is 2× on Flying) | Jirachi — no Fire move on this set, Thunderbolt is neutral and Flash Cannon is resisted |

## Hazard costs per switch — the numbers that decide games

With Stealth Rock and one layer of Spikes against you:

| | SR | Spikes | Per switch |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Clefable** | 0 (Magic Guard) | 0 | **free** |
| Latias | 12.5% | 0 (Levitate) | 12.5% |
| Hippowdon / Jirachi / Magneton | 12.5% | 12.5% | 25% |
| **Gliscor** | **25%** (2× on Flying) | 0 | **25%** |

Clefable is the only free pivot. Gliscor is the most expensive one *and* the Fighting answer —
so it must not be used as a general-purpose switch-in.

## Piloting rules

1. **Lead Hippowdon** and click **Stealth Rock turn 1** unless you are being killed. This team has
   no Rapid Spin; losing the hazard race loses the game.
2. **Use your recovery moves.** Four of six have reliable recovery (Slack Off, Roost, Soft-Boiled,
   Recover). Below ~60% and not about to be KO'd → heal. Clefable especially: Magic Guard means it
   can sit at full forever.
3. **Roar beats setup.** When something Swords Dances or Dragon Dances in front of Hippowdon,
   Roar erases the boost. Do not switch out and let it connect with something squishier.
4. **Magneton is for Skarmory.** Trap it, then Thunderbolt (~89% on a standard spread — Roost heals
   50%, so Skarmory loses that race). Never spend the turn on Thunder Wave against a trapped
   target. Beware Whirlwind: phazing beats Magnet Pull in Gen 4.
5. **Gliscor for Lucario, Latias for Infernape.** Gliscor walls physical Fighting. Latias resists
   Fire Blast, Grass Knot *and* Close Combat — it is the mixed-Infernape answer, so keep it healthy.
6. **Latias cannot switch out with a Pursuit user on the field.** Pursuit doubles and strikes
   before the switch. Attack or stay; never click switch.
7. **Every free switch should be Clefable.**

## Known weaknesses

- **No Rapid Spin** with three grounded members. Use Gliscor's Taunt aggressively on Skarmory and
  Roserade to deny layers.
- **Ice** — Gliscor is 4× weak. Never leave it in on Starmie, Latias or Swampert.
- **Passive.** The win route is hazards + Toxic + paralysis + phazing. There is no sweep. Be
  patient; this team wins long games.

## Design notes

- **Rotom-Heat is the anchor.** In Gen 4 all Rotom formes are **Electric/Ghost** — immune to
  Fighting *and* Normal, immune to Ground via Levitate, and it spinblocks. It is invested specially
  because the physical Fighting move can't touch it at all; Will-O-Wisp covers the physical side.
- **No Rapid Spin, and that's fine.** Four of six ignore hazards: Skarmory (Flying), Rotom-H
  (Levitate), Clefable (Magic Guard), Latias (Levitate). Only Magnezone and Tyranitar are grounded.
- Magnezone traps Steels; Tyranitar's Pursuit handles Latias; Clefable's Seismic Toss handles CM
  Clefable; Calm Mind Latias is the win condition once the Steels are gone.
- **Sharpest test:** Heatran threatens both Steels. Tyranitar's special bulk in sand has to absorb
  Fire Blast — trap and remove before Heatran gets free turns.

## History — why this version exists

The original build was Donphan / Skarmory / Magnezone / Tyranitar / Clefable / Latias and it lost
badly: a single Life Orb Infernape took four Pokémon at the end of the game.

The diagnosis at the time was "zero Fighting resists," which **overstated it** — Latias resists
Fighting (Psychic is 0.5× against it), so there was one. The real problems were:

- Tyranitar **4×** weak to Fighting with no Defence investment
- Clefable and Magnezone both 2×
- The one genuine resist, Latias, died to Scizor's Pursuit before Infernape ever appeared
- Donphan was simultaneously the only spinner and the only Rocks setter, with base 60 SpD
  uninvested — any special Water move removed both roles in one turn (and **Gen 4 Sturdy does not
  save it at 1 HP**)

Swapping Donphan for Rotom-Heat adds a hard Fighting *immunity* and moves Stealth Rock to
Tyranitar. The underlying lesson stands: the original crammed seven jobs (Rocks, Spikes, spin,
Steel-trap, Latias-trap, CM-Clefable answer, win condition) into six slots, and the Fighting answer
was what got squeezed out.
